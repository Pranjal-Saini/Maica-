How it works

Four steps, no admin required.

You can try it on a real problem this afternoon, without asking anyone for permission first.

01

Export what you already see

A saved-search CSV and a System Notes export. Both are things a consultant can produce today — no integration record, no IT ticket, no administrator in the loop.

Handles extra columns, renamed headers, partial date ranges, and different formats in a single upload.

02

Upload it, and keep the evidence

Files are normalised into one shape and stored with their provenance intact, so every later claim can point back at the exact row it came from.

Reports rows_understood and rows_skipped, with a reason for every skip. A file it cannot read is named, not silently dropped.

03

Name the transaction that went wrong

You almost always arrive holding it. Type the ID and the tool gets out of the way — no dashboard to configure, no query to write.

No ID? A shortlist ranks records by how unlike the rest of the account they are, scored on that account's own distribution.

04

Read the factors, and the gaps

Contributing factors ranked by strength of support, each with the evidence behind it, and an explicit list of what could not be checked.

A diagnostic that names its blind spots is more trustworthy, not less.

Features

Built for evidence you did not choose.

Real exports are partial and inconsistent. The engine is designed around that, rather than around a tidy sample.

Two evidence types, one timeline

Saved searches and System Notes normalise into a single record shape, so a configuration change and the script that ran afterwards sit on the same clock. Nothing in the reasoning layer knows which file a row arrived in.

Ranked, not listed

Factors are ordered by strength of support, so the top of the page is where to start rather than whatever sorted first. Ranking uses the account's own distribution, which is what lets it work on accounts customised differently from each other.

Every claim cites its row

Each factor traces back to the exact stored record behind it, so a finding can be checked instead of trusted. The original upload is kept for the same reason — discarding it would make the output unverifiable.

Read-only by construction

There is no code in MAICA that writes to NetSuite. Uploads never touch your account at all, and the planned live connection requests read access only.

What it claims

Never a single verdict.

A component touching a field does not prove it caused the outcome. Every factor carries the strength of its own evidence, in four words that always mean the same thing.

Confirmed

The change itself is documented in the evidence. Not a claim about cause.

Likely

Strongly supported by chronology and direct record relationships, but not proven.

Uncertain

Evidence exists and important gaps remain. The gaps are stated, not glossed.

Insufficient evidence

No reliable conclusion available from what was uploaded. Said plainly.

Access and safety

We cannot modify anything in your account.

True by construction, not by policy. It is the strongest sentence available in a security conversation with a client running production finance, so the product is built to keep it true.

Questions

What people ask first.

Does MAICA connect to my NetSuite account?

Not today. You upload exports you generate yourself, so nothing touches your account and no administrator has to approve anything. A live read-only connection is planned as an upgrade for people who already want it, never as a requirement to start.

Can it change anything in my account?

No, and not as a matter of policy. There is no code in MAICA that writes to NetSuite. Even the planned live connection is read-only, requesting no permission the analysis does not use.

What exactly do I upload?

A saved-search CSV of the transactions you care about, and a System Notes export covering the same period. Both are things a consultant can already produce without special rights.

Extra columns, renamed headers and partial date ranges are all fine. MAICA reports how many rows it understood and how many it skipped, and why.

Will it tell me the root cause?

Deliberately not. It returns contributing factors ranked by the strength of their evidence, and you decide. A script touching a field does not prove it caused the outcome, and a tool that says otherwise is guessing with confidence.

What happens to my client's data?

It is stored so the analysis can cite it, isolated per client account, and treated as confidential regardless of what it contains. You can delete a client account and everything uploaded under it at any time.

The details are in the privacy policy.

Who is it for?

NetSuite consultants — functional and technical — and the administrators and controllers who feel the pain when a transaction posts wrong. It is built for the gap between the two specialisms, so it is most useful when neither person can see the whole picture alone.

What does it cost?

Nothing during the beta. Pricing is not settled, and when it is, it will be aimed at the client or the consulting firm rather than at the individual consultant — the person who saves the time is rarely the person who captures the benefit.

How far along is it?

Beta, and honest about it. Upload, normalisation, the dependency graph, the ranking and the report all work end to end. The live NetSuite connection does not exist yet, and some evidence types are not ingested because their real column names have not been verified against a genuine export.
